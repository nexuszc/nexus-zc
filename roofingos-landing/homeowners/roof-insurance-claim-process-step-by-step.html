Roof Insurance Claim Process: A Step-by-Step Guide for Homeowners

Dealing with roof damage is stressful, and navigating an insurance claim can feel overwhelming. This guide breaks down the roof insurance claim process into clear, manageable steps so you know exactly what to expect and how to protect your interests.

Step 1: Document the Damage Immediately

The moment you notice roof damage—whether from a storm, fallen tree, or other incident—take action right away. Your insurance company will want evidence of what happened and how severe it is.

  • Take clear photos and videos of all damaged areas, including wide shots showing the overall damage pattern
  • Photograph interior damage too (water stains, ceiling leaks, damaged insulation)
  • Write down the date and time the damage occurred, plus weather conditions if relevant
  • Keep receipts for any emergency repairs you make to prevent further damage
  • Don't make permanent repairs yet—the adjuster needs to see the original damage

Save all documentation in a folder or cloud storage so nothing gets lost.

Step 2: Review Your Insurance Policy

Before you call your insurance company, understand what your policy covers. Not all damage is covered equally, and knowing your deductible and coverage limits saves time and prevents surprises.

  • Locate your policy documents and read the roof coverage section carefully
  • Note your deductible amount (what you'll pay out-of-pocket)
  • Check for exclusions—some policies don't cover certain types of damage like wear-and-tear or neglect
  • Look for any special endorsements related to roofing
  • If you're unsure, call your agent and ask specific questions about roof coverage

Step 3: File Your Claim Promptly

Contact your insurance company as soon as possible after discovering damage. Most policies require prompt notification, and delays can complicate your claim.

  • Call the claim number listed on your insurance card or policy documents
  • Provide your policy number, the date damage occurred, and a brief description
  • Ask how to submit your photos and documentation (online portal, email, or mail)
  • Request a claim number and write it down for future reference
  • Ask how long it typically takes for an adjuster to contact you
  • Keep a record of everyone you speak with and when

Step 4: Meet with the Insurance Adjuster

The insurance company will send an adjuster to inspect your roof and assess the damage. This is a critical step—the adjuster's report determines your claim's outcome.

  • Schedule a convenient inspection time and make sure someone is home
  • Point out all damage areas you've documented
  • Be honest and thorough in your descriptions
  • Take notes during the inspection and ask questions about what the adjuster observes
  • Consider hiring your own independent adjuster or having your roofer present if damage is extensive
  • Request a copy of the adjuster's report once it's completed

Step 5: Review the Adjuster's Report and Negotiate if Needed

Once the adjuster submits their report, your insurance company will make an initial offer. This amount may not fully cover your repair costs—and that's where careful review matters.

  • Review the repair estimate provided or get one from a licensed roofer
  • Compare the adjuster's findings to your documentation and photos
  • If estimates don't match, request an explanation from your insurance company
  • You can hire a licensed roofer to dispute the adjuster's assessment
  • If you disagree with the claim decision, ask about your appeal options or the appraisal process

Step 6: Get Repairs Started and Track Progress

Once your claim is approved, hire a reputable roofing contractor to begin work. A good contractor will coordinate with your insurance company and keep you informed every step of the way.
